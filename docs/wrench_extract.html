Picking a block blind from a tight pack — without tearing it off the cup.

A vacuum-cup policy lifts the centre block out of a dense array whose neighbour heights it can't see, carries it across the table, and sets it in a box — while keeping the force on the seal inside the cup's admissible envelope. Trained entirely in a batched MuJoCo-Warp twin of the real arm, it reaches 12 / 12 on unseen geometry with zero seal breaks.

01The task

Nine blocks packed with 2–5 mm gaps. The target is the centre one. The policy is told a noisy belief of the heights — never the truth — so it doesn't know how far it must pull before the block clears its neighbours.

What makes it hard

Pull too little and the block snags a taller neighbour on the way out. Pull too hard, or swing it while carrying, and the transmitted force leaves the cup's admissible wrench set — the seal lets go and the block drops. The safe window is narrow and the geometry changes every scene.

Not a vision model

The policy reads a 33-dimensional state vector — joint angles, tool pose, target-relative offsets, the height belief, and the live wrench reading. On the real arm those entries come from the depth + segmentation stack; in training, injected belief noise stands in for that perception uncertainty.

02The constraint, hands-on

A vacuum cup can only transmit so much before it peels. As the lifting tension (pull) rises toward the cup's limit, the force left over for shear, peel and twist collapses toward zero. Drag the demand point — or raise the pull — and watch the seal margin.

03How it was built

A privileged scripted expert generates the data; behaviour cloning turns it into a stateless policy. Reinforcement learning never produced the final weights — it was the diagnostic that proved the task solvable and exposed the physics.

The pipeline

scripted waypoint teacher
privileged · wrench-governed → behaviour cloning
h=512 · canonical obs · DART noise → stateless policy
obs → action · 12/12

Two negative results shaped this. DAgger consistently hurt: the waypoint teacher's labels are only valid near its own trajectory, so relabelling the policy's drift states fed it systematic garbage — every DAgger round regressed. And PPO fine-tuning overfit or collapsed once the imitation signal was gone. The winning recipe is teacher-only cloning with exploration noise large enough that the teacher demonstrates its own corrections.

Four physics bugs — found by tracing the expert, not tuning the reward

Seven RL runs stalled before the real blockers turned out to be in the simulated contact, not the learning. Tracing the scripted expert and decomposing the interface force exposed each one. Before: 0% of carries survived. After: 128 / 128.

Inverted cup axis

The cup's pointing vector was negated, so tension and compression were swapped — true vacuum pull was never checked against the 18 N limit.

fix use the tool z-axis directly (it already points at the block)

Damper on absolute velocity

The seal's damping acted on world-frame object speed — a 0.5 m/s carry generated ~15 N of phantom drag-shear against an 8.6 N budget, so every transport broke the seal.

fix damp the relative cup–object velocity instead

Double-modelled contact

A rigid cup-tip contact fought the suction spring once sealed — 9 N standing preload, 26 N spikes — manufacturing breaks that no real cup would see.

fix exclude the cup–block contact while the seal is active

Carry snagged the wall

The block hangs ~5 cm below the tool; the box walls top out at 0.25 m, so a low carry path dragged the block into the rim and sheared it off.

fix carry at 0.33 m, above every obstacle

Can it go faster?

The arm isn't slow from caution — in free motion it already uses only ~60% of its speed clamp. The remaining time lives in the seal-limited press and carry, where the margin is the safety buffer. Going faster there means spending exactly the constraint this whole project protects.

06From twin to real arm

The policy is state-based, and its observation maps one-to-one onto the real perception stack — so deployment reuses the controller already proven on this hardware.

Bring-up, verified

MPC stack relaunched on the Pi, feedback streaming at 2 ms latency, a 1° joint probe confirming the drives follow, and object localization from the calibrated fixed depth camera. The streaming controller (real_wrench_ctrl.py) maps sim decisions to the measured velocity-mode drive with the same torque-contact guard and lead-limited reference used in prior real runs.

Safety by construction

Suction releases only when the block is both cleared and over the box — the executor gates the pump the same way the sim does. A joint-space guard box, torque abort, and touch-only demo mode bound every motion; an interrupt stops the stream and drops vacuum.

Appearance is irrelevant to a state-based policy by construction — the showcase run below varies lighting, colour and block palette on top of the geometry randomization, and the behaviour is unchanged: 6 / 6, zero breaks.
